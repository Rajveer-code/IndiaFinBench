\section{Error Analysis}
\label{sec:errortax-sub}

\subsection{Error Taxonomy}

We classify model failures into four interpretable error types via a deterministic mapping from item task type and difficulty (released with the evaluation code): \textbf{Domain Knowledge Failure (DKF)}: unfamiliarity with Indian regulatory concepts, terminology, or thresholds. \textbf{Numerical Reasoning Failure (NRF)}: arithmetic errors---wrong calculation, unit conversion, or formula application despite the formula appearing in context. \textbf{Temporal Reasoning Failure (TRF)}: mis-ordering regulatory events, misidentifying the circular in force at a given time, or failing to track supersession. \textbf{Context Grounding Failure (CGF)}: using external knowledge instead of the passage, or failing to extract a span clearly present in context. Contradiction-detection errors on medium and hard items map to TRF, since the task probes supersession tracking; easy CON items and hard REG items both map to CGF.

Table~\ref{tab:errortax} shows error distributions for five key models under both scoring regimes: strict-pipeline errors, and residual errors that survive the cross-model judge audit (phi4-mini, Section~\ref{sec:judge}, Appendix~\ref{app:judge}). The distinction matters because strict errors mix true failures with format mismatches, whereas residual errors are judge-retained.

Four patterns emerge. First, temporal-supersession failures (TRF) dominate the residual, judge-retained error set for every profiled model---from 50\% (Gemini 2.5 Flash: 7/14) to 62\% (LLaMA-3.3-70B: 5/8, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-70B: 5/8, Gemma 3 4B: 15/24)---regardless of overall model tier: the judge audit consistently clears numerical and domain-knowledge format mismatches while leaving supersession-tracking errors largely intact. Second, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-70B shows the largest correction: 101 strict failures (across REG, NUM, TMP, and CON) collapse to 8 residual---6 of which are the REG/NUM/TMP residual failures behind the 93.9\% reclassification figure in Section~\ref{sec:regime}, the remaining 2 are CON errors, which are never judge-reviewed---the largest reclassification of any profiled model and the basis for its rank reversal. Third, Gemma 3 4B's errors also fall substantially under judging (86 $\rightarrow$ 24, a 72.1\% reclassification rate), so its strict-error count no longer supports a claim that its errors are format-invariant; what does survive is its residual \emph{rate}: at 24/406 = 5.9\% of all items, it remains 1.7--3$\times$ every other profiled model's residual rate (2.0--3.4\%), concentrated in numerical reasoning (6 residual NRF errors, the largest absolute NRF count of the five). Fourth, Gemini 2.5 Flash---the top strict performer---has the \emph{lowest} reclassification rate of the five (66.7\%): proportionally more of its strict failures are judge-retained as genuine, consistent with its output already being well-formatted under strict scoring, leaving fewer format artifacts available to reclassify in the first place. Context Grounding Failure is rare under both regimes for every model.
