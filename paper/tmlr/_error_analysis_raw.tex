\section{Error Analysis}
\label{sec:errortax-sub}

\subsection{Task-Aligned Error Attribution}

We attribute model failures to four categories via a deterministic mapping from each item's task
type and difficulty (released with the evaluation code): \textbf{Domain Knowledge Failure (DKF)}:
unfamiliarity with Indian regulatory concepts, terminology, or thresholds. \textbf{Numerical
Reasoning Failure (NRF)}: arithmetic errors---wrong calculation, unit conversion, or formula
application despite the formula appearing in context. \textbf{Temporal Reasoning Failure (TRF)}:
mis-ordering regulatory events, misidentifying the circular in force at a given time, or failing to
track supersession. \textbf{Context Grounding Failure (CGF)}: using external knowledge instead of
the passage, or failing to extract a span clearly present in context. Contradiction-detection errors
on medium and hard items map to TRF, since the task probes supersession tracking; easy CON items and
hard REG items both map to CGF. \textbf{These labels are task-conditioned categories, not
diagnoses of a model's internal failure mechanism}: because the mapping is deterministic in task
type and difficulty, an error's label carries no information beyond which task and difficulty tier
it occurred in. A pattern stated purely in terms of these labels (e.g.\ ``TRF is common'') is a
restatement of which task the errors fall in, not an independent finding about that task; the
findings below that carry independent information are the ones stated in terms of per-model
\emph{reclassification rates}, not error-type composition.

Table~\ref{tab:errortax} shows error distributions for five key models under both scoring regimes: strict-pipeline errors, and residual errors that survive the cross-model judge audit (phi4-mini, Section~\ref{sec:judge}, Appendix~\ref{app:judge}). The distinction matters because strict errors mix true failures with format mismatches, whereas residual errors are judge-retained.

Three patterns carry independent information, stated as per-model reclassification rates rather
than error-type composition. First, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-70B shows the largest correction: 101
strict failures (across REG, NUM, TMP, and CON) collapse to 8 residual---6 of which are the
REG/NUM/TMP residual failures behind the 93.9\% reclassification figure in
Section~\ref{sec:regime}, the remaining 2 are CON errors, which are never judge-reviewed---the
largest reclassification of any profiled model and the basis for its rank reversal. Second, Gemma
3 4B's errors also fall substantially under judging (86 $\rightarrow$ 24, a 72.1\% reclassification
rate), so its strict-error count no longer supports a claim that its errors are format-invariant;
what does survive is its residual \emph{rate}: at 24/406 = 5.9\% of all items, it remains
1.7--3$\times$ every other profiled model's residual rate (2.0--3.4\%). Third, Gemini
2.5 Flash---the top strict performer---has the \emph{lowest} reclassification rate of the five
(66.7\%): proportionally more of its strict failures are judge-retained as genuine, consistent with
its output already being well-formatted under strict scoring, leaving fewer format artifacts
available to reclassify in the first place.

A task-level pattern is also visible in the residual composition, with the caveat above that it
restates task and difficulty rather than adding independent information: items whose deterministic
label is TRF (chiefly temporal-reasoning items, plus medium/hard contradiction-detection items) make
up 50\% (Gemini 2.5 Flash: 7/14) to 62\% (LLaMA-3.3-70B: 5/8, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-70B: 5/8,
Gemma 3 4B: 15/24) of every profiled model's residual, judge-retained errors, while items labelled
NRF or DKF are cleared at a substantially higher rate under judging. Read as a task-level fact rather
than a mechanistic one, this says supersession-tracking items resist the judge's format-tolerance
more than numerical or domain-knowledge items do, for every model profiled here --- not that models
share a specific ``temporal reasoning'' failure mode. Gemma 3 4B's residual concentration noted above
is, by the same deterministic mapping, concentrated in items labelled NRF (6 of its 24 residual
errors, the largest absolute NRF count of the five). Context Grounding Failure is rare under both
regimes for every model.
