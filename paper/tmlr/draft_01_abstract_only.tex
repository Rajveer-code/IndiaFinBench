\begin{abstract}
A benchmark leaderboard reports one accuracy number, but that number is produced by a scoring rule
as much as by a model. We ask what changes when only the rule changes. IndiaFinBench, introduced
here, is a 406-item expert-annotated benchmark over SEBI and RBI regulatory text covering
regulatory interpretation, numerical reasoning, contradiction detection, and temporal reasoning; it
is built on a corpus where models cannot fall back on memorised Western financial text, which makes
the comparison sharper. We evaluate twelve contemporary LLMs zero-shot under two scoring regimes: a
strict four-stage string-matching pipeline, and an item-level judge audit of all 874 flagged errors,
itself checked against manual review (89.3\% agreement on a 28-item stratified audit).

The regimes disagree, and the disagreement is not cosmetic. Strict accuracy spans 70.4--89.7\%;
judge-corrected accuracy spans 79.8--96.6\%. DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-70B, a reasoning-distilled model, ranks
12th of twelve (last place) under strict scoring and 1st under judge-corrected scoring, because
93.9\% of its strict errors are format non-compliance rather than wrong reasoning. Nine of the
twelve models move by two or more ranks, and the two orderings are only weakly related (Spearman
$\rho = 0.413$,
$p = 0.18$), so a strict leaderboard says little about which model reasons better. The correction
also compresses the field: excluding the weakest model, the remaining eleven span 14.6 points under
strict scoring and 6.0 under corrected scoring. Most of the apparent capability gap between
mid-table models is format compliance.

Item-level analysis compounds the problem. At least 11 of the 12 models answer 213 of the 406 items
correctly, so those items separate almost no pair of systems; the benchmark's discriminative signal
rests on 134 items. Nominal size and effective size are not the same quantity, and benchmarks
rarely report the difference.

We also anchor the scale against a person. On a 60-item subset drawn from the medium and hard
strata, one non-specialist evaluator scored 80.0\% (48/60; 95\% Wilson CI [68.2, 88.2]). This is a
single evaluator and a reference point, not a population estimate: no model is significantly better,
and only the weakest is significantly worse. Dataset, evaluation harness, per-item judge verdicts,
and all model predictions are released.
\end{abstract}
